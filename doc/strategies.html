Setup

The Universe, M and N

Every strategy starts by ranking the entire eligible universe by momentum score at each rebalance date.

📊 The Ranked Universe

At every rebalance, all eligible stocks are sorted by their composite Sharpe score — best at the top, worst at the bottom.

M — Entry Threshold

The portfolio targets the top M stocks. A stock ranked ≤ M is eligible to enter. Typical value: M = 15.

N — Exit Threshold (N > M)

A stock only exits when it drops below rank N. The gap (N − M) creates a buffer zone where stocks are safe from being sold. Typical value: N = 20.

Why a buffer? Ranking fluctuates week-to-week due to noise. Without the gap, stocks near rank M would churn in and out constantly, generating unnecessary taxes and brokerage.

Band Rule 1 of 2

Classic Band Rule

Aggressive entry — always tries to hold ALL top-M stocks regardless of what is already in the portfolio.

📤 Exit Rule

A stock exits if its rank drops below N (outside the top-N universe). Both top-M and buffer-zone stocks are protected from selling as long as they stay within top-N.

📥 Entry Rule

Every stock ranked ≤ M that isn't already in the portfolio enters — regardless of how many slots are free. The portfolio temporarily expands above M if buffer-zone stocks are still held.

Key behaviour At each rebalance: exits happen first, then ALL top-M newcomers are added. If a buffer-zone stock didn't exit, the portfolio size briefly exceeds M. The equal-weight scheme (Full variant) then normalises this.
Implication on turnover Potentially high. Every time a new stock enters top-M, it automatically joins the portfolio even if there's no vacancy.
Classic logic — step by step
Step 1 — Identify exits
Remove every held stock that is not in top_n (rank > N).
exits = current_holdings − top_n
Step 2 — Identify entries
Add every stock from top_m not yet in the portfolio. No slot-count check.
entries = top_m − current_holdings
Step 3 — New holdings
new = (current_holdings − exits) ∪ entries
⚠ size may exceed M temporarily

Band Rule 2 of 2

Displacement Band Rule

Conservative entry — new stocks can only enter when a slot is freed. The portfolio never exceeds M.

📤 Exit Rule (same as Classic)

A stock exits if its rank drops below N. Identical to Classic — the buffer zone protects against whipsaw selling.

📥 Entry Rule — DIFFERENT

New entrants can only fill freed slots (slots vacated by exits). If no stocks exited this period, no new stocks enter — even if top-M has better-ranked candidates outside the portfolio.

Key behaviour The portfolio is treated like a fixed-capacity club. A new member can only join when someone leaves. Buffer-zone incumbents are never displaced by higher-ranked outsiders.
Implication on turnover Lower turnover than Classic. Stocks held in the buffer zone are never sold just to make room for new top-M entrants. Each trade has a definitive reason: an exit opened a slot.
Displacement logic — step by step
Step 1 — Identify exits (same rule)
exits = current_holdings − top_n
Step 2 — Count freed slots
free_slots = M − (|current_holdings| − |exits|)
Step 3 — Fill freed slots only
Candidates come from top-M stocks not already held. Sort by rank. Take the best up to free_slots.
entries = best(top_m − holdings_after_exits)
         [:free_slots] ← hard cap
Step 4 — New holdings
new = (current_holdings − exits) ∪ entries
✓ size ≤ M always

Band Rules — Head to Head

Classic vs Displacement: The Key Differences

Same exit rule. Radically different entry behaviour. Everything downstream flows from this choice.

🔵 Classic Band

Entry: "All top-M, unconditionally"

Every stock ranked ≤ M that isn't held joins the portfolio. No slot check. Portfolio can exceed M.

Portfolio size

Varies. Can be larger than M when buffer-zone stocks are still held alongside new top-M entrants. Normalises toward M as buffer stocks gradually exit.

Best for…

Environments where momentum is persistent and quick rotation into new leaders adds alpha. Accepts higher turnover for faster repositioning.

Turnover profile: Higher New top-M entrants always join, even if no one exited. Incumbent positions reweight each rebalance.
🟣 Displacement Band

Entry: "Fill freed slots only"

New stocks enter only when a slot opens (someone fell below N). Entries are always bounded by exits. Portfolio never exceeds M.

Portfolio size

Strictly ≤ M at all times. Starts below M and fills as exits create vacancies. Hard cap enforced in code.

Best for…

Tax-sensitive portfolios and slower rebalance frequencies. Incumbents get a long runway; only genuine rank deterioration (below N) triggers a sale.

Turnover profile: Lower Entries ≤ exits by construction. A period with zero exits = zero entries = zero trades = zero costs.
Rebalance Method 1 of 3

Full Rebalance

Every rebalance date: sell all, buy fresh. Each holding resets to an exactly equal slice of the portfolio.

⚙️ Mechanism

After the band rule decides which stocks to hold, every holding — new entrants and incumbents — receives equal weight 1 / portfolio_size. This resets price drift every period.

📐 Formula

w(stock) = 1 / |new_holdings|
for every stock in portfolio

Cost impact: Highest Every rebalance touches every position. Winners that grew large are trimmed; laggards that shrank are topped up. This "sell-high, buy-low" behaviour inside the portfolio incurs maximum brokerage and capital-gains events.
Advantage The portfolio always enters each period exactly as intended. No position drift, no hidden concentration risk. Clean and predictable.

Rebalance Method 2 of 3

Slot-Fill (Marginal) Rebalance

Incumbents keep their drifted weights. The capital freed by exiting stocks is split equally among new entrants.

⚙️ Mechanism

Positions that stay in the portfolio are not touched — their weights drift with price. When stocks exit, their current weight is pooled and divided equally among the new entrants.

📐 Formula

incumbents: keep drifted weight
freed_w = Σ drifted_w(exits)
each new entrant gets: freed_w / |entries|
then: normalise all to sum = 1

Cost impact: Lower Only exiting stocks are fully sold. Incumbents are never touched. Fewer trades, lower brokerage, fewer CGT events on existing positions.
Trade-off Over time, winner positions accumulate concentration risk — a stock that has risen 3× since entry will have a much larger weight than intended. Periodically a Full Rebalance resets this.

Slot-Fill — Edge Case (Classic Band Only)

Marginal: New Entry With No Exit

What weight does a new entrant get when nobody exited? The freed capital is zero — so the fallback rule kicks in.

🤔 When does this happen?

Only with the Classic band rule. Classic adds every top-M stock that isn't held, even if no one exits. Displacement can never produce entries without exits (entries ≤ freed slots by construction).

⚙️ The fallback rule (line 824)

freed_slot = Σ drifted_w(exits) = 0
per_entry = freed / entries → 1 / size
(fallback because freed_slot = 0)
then: normalise all to sum = 1

Effect on incumbents No capital was freed, but a new position is added. The total weight before normalisation exceeds 1.0. Normalisation implicitly dilutes every incumbent proportionally — a hidden "soft trim" of all existing positions.
Slot-Fill = Prop-Fill here Both methods seed new entrants at 1/size when there are no exits. The two strategies produce identical weights in this scenario. Their difference only shows up when exits have unequal drifted weights.

Rebalance Method 3 of 3

Prop-Fill Rebalance

Incumbents keep their drifted weights. New entrants are seeded at a fixed 1/size fraction, then everything is normalised.

⚙️ Mechanism

Like Marginal, incumbents are not traded. But new entrants are seeded at a fixed 1/size weight regardless of the freed capital. The total is then normalised back to 1.0.

📐 Formula

incumbents: keep drifted weight
each new entrant seeded at: 1 / |new_holdings|
then: normalise all to sum = 1

Key difference from Slot-Fill In Slot-Fill, the new entrant's weight = freed capital (depends on what exited). In Prop-Fill, the seed is fixed at 1/size — independent of exit weights. Normalisation then redistributes the difference across everyone.
When it matters If a large position (e.g. 45% drifted) exits and one stock enters: Slot-Fill gives it 45%. Prop-Fill seeds at 1/size (say 33%), then normalises — incumbents absorb the difference. New entrant gets a more moderate starting position.

Rebalance Methods — Head to Head

Full vs Marginal vs Prop: What Changes

The band rule decides who is in the portfolio. The rebalance method decides how much each stock gets.

🔵 Full Rebalance

What changes each period

Every position. Winners trimmed, laggards topped up, new entrants funded from all proceeds.

Incumbent weight

Resets to 1 / size. Drift is completely erased every rebalance.

New entrant weight

Starts at 1 / size — same as everyone else.

Turnover: Highest Every rebalance touches all positions.
🟢 Slot-Fill (Marginal)

What changes each period

Only exiting stocks are sold. Incumbents are never traded unless they exit.

Incumbent weight

Drifts with price. A stock that rallied 50% since entry now has a proportionally larger weight — no trade to reset it.

New entrant weight

Receives exactly the freed weight of exits (split equally if multiple entrants).

Turnover: Lowest Zero trades when there are no entries/exits.
🟣 Prop-Fill

What changes each period

Only exiting stocks are sold. Incumbents drift. Normalisation causes a small implicit reweight on incumbents.

Incumbent weight

Drifts, then is gently nudged by normalisation when entrants are seeded.

New entrant weight

Seeded at 1 / size, then normalised. Typically gives entrants a slightly smaller allocation than Slot-Fill.

Turnover: Low Similar to Marginal; slightly more implicit reweighting due to normalisation.

🔵 Classic
Classic · Full
All top-M always in. Equal weights. Max turnover, max precision. Best theoretical benchmark — shows "pure" strategy performance ignoring costs.
Classic · Marginal
All top-M enter; incumbents accumulate drift. Practical for holding periods where reweighting is expensive. Watch for concentration risk.
Classic · Prop
All top-M enter with normalised seeding. New entrants get a moderate position rather than inheriting a large exiting stock's full weight.
🟣 Displacement
Displacement · Full
Only freed-slot entries; equal weights on every rebalance. Lower entry frequency than Classic · Full, but same full-portfolio reweight cost each period.
Displacement · Marginal
Fewest trades of all 6. Incumbents drift; new stocks only enter to fill exact vacancies. Best for minimising CGT events — ideal for long-hold tax-sensitive use.
Displacement · Prop
Conservative entry + proportional seeding. New entrant size anchored to 1/size rather than inherited from exit. Balanced between Marginal and Full entry sizing.
💡 Cost drag ranking (highest → lowest) Classic·Full > Displ·Full > Classic·Prop ≈ Classic·Marginal > Displ·Prop ≈ Displ·Marginal
✅ For tax-sensitive Indian portfolios Displacement·Marginal minimises both brokerage and CGT-triggering sales. Displacement·Prop is a good middle ground.
